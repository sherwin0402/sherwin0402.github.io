## Professional Summary
I am a final-year Ph.D. student at Nanyang Technological University (NTU), Singapore, advised by Professor Ang Wei Tech, and expect to complete my Ph.D. by February 2026. My thesis, *Human-in-the-Loop Simulation for Adaptive Assistive Robots: Personalising Human Models and Robot Control*, explores physics-based simulation of physical human-robot interaction (pHRI), particularly in assistive and rehabilitative contexts. Using MuJoCo, I have modeled and evaluated interaction dynamics across various systems, including robot-assisted feeding arms, gait-assist devices, and upper- and lower-limb exoskeletons.

A core focus of my Ph.D. was developing digital human twins that capture individual abilities or disabilities and co-adapt with robots in simulation to create realistic, personalized representations of user behavior and adaptation in physically interactive scenarios. To date, I have authored or co-authored six peer-reviewed papers—including publications at ICRA, IROS, and other top robotics venues—demonstrating the application of these methods across assistive feeding, gait assistance, and human-robot simulation frameworks.

Beyond technical research, I have developed strong collaborative and leadership skills through guiding seven final-year project (FYP) students and working closely with cross-functional teams spanning biomechanics, clinical therapy, engineering, and academic research. I am actively involved in research strategy and grant writing, contributing to funding proposals ranging from smaller SGD 200K grants to multi-institutional proposals exceeding SGD 3 million.

## What's Next?
As I transition out of my Ph.D., I’m excited to join teams solving hard problems in embodied AI, simulation, or human-centered robotics—whether in research, development, or open-source ecosystems. I personally hope that my work contributes toward a broader vision of robotics systems that are personalized, interpretable, and grounded in real-world human needs.

## Current and Emerging Directions
I am also building soft-body models for higher fidelity and realism in physical human-robot interaction and investigating how foundation models (FMs) can enhance simulation fidelity and personalization—improving a robot’s understanding of human intent and enabling generalization across users and tasks.

This includes expanding into humanoid platforms to support more sophisticated locomotion and manipulation, and exploring vision-language models (VLMs/VLAs) and large language models (LLMs) for both physics-based simulation and real-world human-robot interaction. I am actively developing new workflows in IsaacLab to support this next generation of intelligent, FM-driven robotic behavior.

My current and emerging directions remain broad, as I am deeply fascinated by many aspects of robotics and embodied AI—from perception and planning to simulation and interaction. Underlying this curiosity is a fundamental belief: robotics should ultimately be useful for people. I am motivated by the potential of robots to enhance human capability, safety, and quality of life in real-world settings.

## Personal Interests
Outside of work, I enjoy 3D printing, designing models, mountain biking, and gaming. I’m also experimenting with self-hosted LLMs to streamline personal and investing workflows—an extension of my interest in intelligent systems. Lately, I’ve been inspired by open-source robotics initiatives like Arctos and OpenArm, and have started building a 3D-printed robotic arm at home for fun. I also enjoy reading about geopolitics, economics, and culture to broaden my perspective beyond tech.
