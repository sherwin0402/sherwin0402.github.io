I am a 4th year Ph.D. student at the Assistive Robotics Lab, Nanyang Technological University, Singapore, supervised by Professor Ang Wei Tech. My research focuses on physics-based simulation of physical human-robot interaction (pHRI), with an emphasis on assistive and rehabilitative devices. I primarily use MuJoCo to model and evaluate these interactions, and have simulated various robotic systems including robot-assisted feeding arms, lower-limb exoskeletons, gait-assistive robots, and upper-limb exoskeletons.

A core focus of my Ph.D. is developing digital twins that accurately capture the user’s abilities or disabilities, while also enabling human models to co-adapt with the robot in simulation. This allows for realistic, personalized representations of user behavior and adaptation in physically interactive scenarios. I am also developing soft body human models and exploring how foundation models can enhance simulation by improving the robot’s understanding of human intent and generalization across users and tasks. My work is expanding toward humanoid form factors to support more advanced locomotion and manipulation.

Broadly, my interests include human-robot interaction, assistive robotics, digital human modeling, and simulation-driven methods for safe, intelligent, and personalized robot behavior. Beyond my research, I enjoy a range of creative hobbies. I spend my free time 3D printing, designing 3D models, mountain biking, and computer gaming. These hobbies allow me to explore different forms of creativity and unwind from my academic pursuits. -->
