Sherwin Stephen Chan (陈健怡)

博士最后一年研究生（预计2026年2月毕业） | 南洋理工大学，新加坡 | RRIS，新加坡康复研究院

我是新加坡南洋理工大学（NTU）的博士最后一年研究生，导师为洪伟德（Ang Wei Tech）教授，预计将于2026年2月完成博士学位。我的论文题目为 Human-in-the-Loop Simulation for Adaptive Assistive Robots: Personalising Human Models and Robot Control，主要探索基于物理的人机交互（pHRI）仿真，特别是在辅助和康复领域的应用。我使用MuJoCo对各种系统的交互动力学进行建模和评估，包括机器人辅助喂食机械臂、步态辅助设备以及上下肢外骨骼。

我博士研究的核心是开发数字人体孪生，能够捕捉个体的能力或残疾，并在仿真中与机器人共同适应，从而在物理交互场景中创建真实且个性化的用户行为和适应表现。迄今为止，我已发表或合作发表了六篇同行评议论文——包括在ICRA、IROS等顶级机器人学会议上的发表——展示了这些方法在辅助喂食、步态辅助和人机交互仿真框架中的应用。

除了技术研究外，我还通过指导七名本科毕业设计学生，以及与跨越生物力学、临床治疗、工程和学术研究等跨职能团队的密切合作，培养了强大的协作和领导技能。我积极参与研究策略制定和资助申请撰写，为从小型的20万新元资助到超过300万新元的多机构合作项目提供支持。

未来展望

随着博士学位的即将完成，我期待加入专注于解决具身人工智能、仿真或以人为中心的机器人技术难题的团队——无论是在研究、开发还是开源生态系统中。我个人希望我的工作能为更广阔的机器人系统愿景做出贡献，即构建个性化、可解释且植根于现实世界人类需求的机器人系统。

当前和新兴研究方向

我还在构建柔体模型以提高物理人机交互的保真度和真实性，并研究基础模型（FMs）如何增强仿真保真度和个性化——提高机器人对人类意图的理解并实现跨用户、跨任务的泛化能力。

这包括扩展到类人形平台以支持更复杂的运动和操作，以及探索视觉-语言模型（VLMs/VLAs）和大语言模型（LLMs）在基于物理的仿真和现实世界人机交互中的应用。我正在积极开发IsaacLab中的新工作流程，以支持下一代智能、基础模型驱动的机器人行为。

我当前和新兴的研究方向仍然广泛，因为我对机器人学和具身人工智能的许多方面都深感着迷——从感知和规划到仿真和交互。支撑这种好奇心的是一个基本信念：机器人最终应该对人类有用。我的动力来源于机器人在现实世界环境中增强人类能力、安全性和生活质量的潜力。

个人兴趣

工作之余，我享受3D打印、设计模型、山地骑行和游戏。我还在实验使用自托管的大语言模型来简化个人和投资工作流程——这是我对智能系统兴趣的延伸。最近，我受到Arctos和OpenArm等开源机器人倡议的启发，开始在家构建一个3D打印的机器人手臂作为娱乐项目。我也喜欢阅读关于地缘政治、经济学和文化的内容，以拓宽我在技术之外的视野。

  2. IEEE ICRA
    A Human-In-The-Loop Simulation Framework for Evaluating Control Strategies in Gait Assistive Robots
    Yifan Wang*, Sherwin Stephen Chan*, Mingyuan Lei, Lek Syn Lim, and 3 more authors
    2025 IEEE International Conference on Robotics and Automation (ICRA), 2025
    * Denotes equal contribution
    Abs Video

    As the global population ages, effective rehabilitation and mobility aids will become increasingly critical. Gait assistive robots are promising solutions, but designing adaptable controllers for various impairments poses a significant challenge. This paper presented a Human-In-The-Loop (HITL) simulation framework tailored specifically for gait assistive robots, addressing unique challenges posed by passive support systems. We incorporated a realistic physical human-robot interaction (pHRI) model to enable a quantitative evaluation of robot control strategies, highlighting the performance of a speedadaptive controller compared to a conventional PID controller in maintaining compliance and reducing gait distortion. We assessed the accuracy of the simulated interactions against that of the real-world data and revealed discrepancies in the adaptation strategies taken by the human and their effect on the human’s gait. This work underscored the potential of HITL simulation as a versatile tool for developing and fine-tuning personalized control policies for various users.

  3. IEEE ICORR
    Creation and evaluation of human models with varied walking ability from motion capture for assistive device development
    Sherwin Stephen Chan, Mingyuan Lei, Henry Johan, and Wei Tech Ang
    In 2023 International Conference on Rehabilitation Robotics (ICORR), 2023
    Abs Video

    As the world ages, rehabilitation and assistive devices will play a key role in improving mobility. However, designing controllers for these devices presents several challenges, from varying degrees of impairment to unique adaptation strategies of users. To use computer simulation to address these challenges, simulating human motions is required. Recently, deep reinforcement learning (DRL) has been successfully applied to generate walking motions whose goal is to produce a stable human walking policy. However, from a rehabilitation perspective, it is more important to match the walking policy’s ability to that of an impaired person with reduced ability. In this paper, we present the first attempt to investigate the correlation between DRL training parameters with the ability of the generated human walking policy to recover from perturbation. We show that the control policies can produce gait patterns resembling those of humans without perturbation and that varying perturbation parameters during training can create variation in the recovery ability of the human model. We also demonstrate that the control policy can produce similar behaviours when subjected to forces that users may experience while using a balance assistive device.
